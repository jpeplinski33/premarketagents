# Pre-market vs MLS: what buyers should know

If you’ve been house hunting for more than a few weeks, you’ve probably heard the term “pre-market” tossed around. Maybe a friend mentioned a “pocket listing,” or you saw a sign in a yard that said “coming soon.” But what does pre-market actually mean, and how is it different from the MLS?

The short answer: pre-market is the period before a home is officially listed on the Multiple Listing Service (MLS). The MLS is the database most agents use to share active listings with the public. Both have a role in how homes are bought and sold, but they work very differently—and knowing the difference can save you time, money, and frustration.

Let’s break it down calmly and clearly.

---

## What is the MLS?

The MLS is a shared database that real estate agents use to list properties for sale. When a home is “on the MLS,” it means the listing is visible to all participating agents, and through syndication, it appears on public sites like Zillow, Redfin, and Realtor.com.

Key features of an MLS listing:

- **Public visibility** – Once live, the listing is broadcast to hundreds of websites within hours.
- **Standardized data** – Square footage, beds, baths, lot size, and photos are all entered in a consistent format.
- **Agent cooperation** – Other agents can schedule showings, submit offers, and negotiate on behalf of their buyers.
- **Status updates** – The listing moves through statuses: active, pending, contingent, sold.

For buyers, the MLS is the most common place to search. It’s reliable, searchable, and updated regularly. But it’s also the most competitive—because everyone sees the same homes at the same time.

---

## What is pre-market?

Pre-market refers to homes that are not yet on the MLS. They may be:

- **Coming soon** – The seller has signed a listing agreement, but the home hasn’t gone live yet.
- **Private or off-market** – The home is for sale, but only a select group of agents and buyers know about it.
- **Not yet listed** – The seller is still preparing the home, but has agreed to show it to a few qualified buyers.

Pre-market homes are sometimes called “pocket listings” or “whisper listings.” The common thread is that they are not publicly advertised.

Why would a seller choose pre-market?

- **Privacy** – High-profile sellers, or those who don’t want neighbors or tenants to know, may prefer a quiet sale.
- **Less disruption** – No open houses, no constant showings, no “for sale” sign in the yard.
- **Testing the market** – A seller might want to gauge interest before committing to a full listing.
- **Speed or convenience** – If a seller is relocating quickly, they might prefer a direct sale.

---

## Key differences at a glance

| Aspect | MLS Listing | Pre-Market |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| Visibility | Public, syndicated to major sites | Private, invite-only |
| Competition | High – many buyers see it | Low – only a few know |
| Showings | Scheduled through agents, often open | Often by appointment, sometimes limited |
| Negotiation | Standard process, multiple offers possible | More flexible, but less transparent |
| Timeline | Fixed listing period | Can be flexible or urgent |

---

## Why pre-market can be attractive to buyers

The biggest appeal of pre-market is **less competition**. If you’re the only buyer who knows about a home, you’re not bidding against ten other families. That can lead to:

- More time to think and negotiate
- Less pressure to waive contingencies
- A better chance of getting your offer accepted

Pre-market can also give you a **head start**. If a home is coming soon, you can tour it, do your due diligence, and have an offer ready before it ever hits the public market. In a hot market, that can be the difference between getting a home and missing out.

---

## The catch: pre-market is invite-only

Here’s the important part: pre-market is not something you can browse on a public website. There’s no “pre-market filter” on Zillow. These homes are shared through private networks—usually between agents who know each other, or through exclusive buyer groups.

That means you need to be “in the room” to know about them. How do you get in the room?

- **Work with a well-connected agent** – Agents who have been in the business for a while often hear about homes before they’re listed.
- **Ask your agent to set up a pre-market alert** – Some brokerages have internal lists of off-market properties.
- **Build relationships with local agents** – If you’ve toured homes with an agent, they may think of you when they hear of a private sale.

Also, be aware that some pre-market listings are shared with **full maps, owner intel, and private galleries**—but only to invited buyers or agents. That information is not public. It’s a privilege, not a right, and it’s usually reserved for serious, qualified buyers.

---

## Risks and limitations of pre-market

Pre-market isn’t always better. There are trade-offs:

- **Less transparency** – You may not have access to the same disclosures or inspection reports as you would with an MLS listing.
- **Pricing uncertainty** – Without public comparables, it’s harder to know if the asking price is fair.
- **Limited options** – Pre-market homes are rare. You can’t rely on them as your only strategy.
- **No public record** – If you buy pre-market, the final sale price may not appear in public records right away, which can affect future appraisals.

Also, some sellers use pre-market as a test. If they don’t get the offer they want, they may pull the home and list it on the MLS later—at a higher price. So you might not get a “deal,” just a quieter process.

---

## How to use both strategies together

Smart buyers don’t choose between pre-market and MLS—they use both.

1. **Start with the MLS** to understand the market, pricing, and neighborhoods.
2. **Ask your agent about pre-market opportunities** in your target area.
3. **Be ready to act quickly** if a pre-market home comes your way. That means having your financing pre-approved, your documents ready, and your criteria clear.

The MLS gives you breadth. Pre-market gives you depth. Together, they give you the best chance of finding the right home at the right price.

---

## Three practical takeaways

1. **Pre-market is not a secret club—it’s a private network.** You can access it, but only through a licensed agent who knows the local market and has relationships with other agents.
2. **Don’t skip the MLS.** It’s still the most reliable source of active listings, pricing data, and market trends. Pre-market is a supplement, not a replacement.
3. **Ask the right questions.** When you talk to an agent, ask: “Do you have access to pre-market or off-market listings in my area? How do you hear about them?” A good agent will be honest about what they can and cannot share.

---

## A soft note on next steps

If you’re curious about pre-market opportunities in your area, the best next step is to have a conversation with a licensed realtor. They can explain how pre-market works locally, what’s available, and whether it makes sense for your situation. No pressure—just a clear, honest discussion about your options.

Pre-market and MLS are two sides of the same coin. Understanding both puts you in a stronger position, whether you’re buying your first home or your fifth.
